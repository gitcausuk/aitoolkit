Oxford summative assessment formats
All 40 formats with proportionate direct-evidence design strategies
Purpose: Use this reference when selecting or strengthening an Oxford summative assessment format. The strategies focus on making the intended knowledge, reasoning, judgement, process or
performance visible. No format, process record or oral component automatically proves authorship or eliminates unauthorised assistance. Where AI could materially obscure the capability
being assessed, use more than one proportionate source of evidence and ensure any additional component is specified in advance, accessible, consistently applied and locally approved.

Formats 11-15
Format | Type | Task | Description | Proportionate direct-evidence design strategies
Written exam Online, open book, timed
(up to 3 hours)
Essays A short piece of writing on a specific
topic or subject.
Open-book conditions may make AI accessible. Declare AI permissions clearly in advance.
Design prompts that require synthesis of named course sources, application to supplied
material and a justified critical position. These strategies strengthen evidence of judgement
but do not make the assessment AI-proof or enforce a prohibition on their own.
Written exam Online, open book, timed
(up to 3 hours)
Short-answer questions Open-ended questions that assess
knowledge and understanding.
Students may be required to
complete a sentence, provide a short
descriptive answer or annotate a
diagram.
Use supplied passages, data or sources and ask students to annotate, interpret or apply
relevant frameworks. Require concise justification where reasoning is part of the intended
learning. Declare AI permissions clearly in advance. Course-specific or recent material
supports relevance but does not by itself verify authorship.
Written exam Online, open book, timed
(up to 3 hours)
Case study response Students respond to a real-world
problem or challenge; this may
involve material released in advance
which the student has the opportunity
to review and prepare notes on.
Ground the case in specific supplied evidence and a defined decision context. Require a
prioritised response, explicit reasoning, assumptions and trade-offs. Declare AI permissions
clearly in advance. Local or novel scenarios can improve authenticity but should not be
treated as proof that AI assistance was unavailable.
Written exam Online, open book,
window (2 or 3 day)
Essays A short piece of writing on a specific
topic or subject.
An extended window can weaken the inference that the submission alone evidences the
student's capability. Declare AI permissions before the window opens. Strengthen the task
through direct engagement with supplied evidence and use proportionate process evidence
only where it contributes to validity. An oral follow-up should be planned, accessible,
consistently applied and locally approved; no component proves authorship.
Written exam Online, open book,
window (2 or 3 day)
Short-answer questions Open-ended questions that assess
knowledge and understanding.
Students may be required to
complete a sentence, provide a short
descriptive answer or annotate a
diagram.
Use coherent multi-part questions that require a consistent thread of reasoning, direct
engagement with supplied material and explicit justification. Declare AI permissions clearly in
advance. Teaching-unit-specific or unpublished material may support relevance but should
not be treated as proof of independent authorship.

Written exam Online, open book,
window (2 or 3 day)
Case study response Students respond to a real-world
problem or challenge; this may
involve material released in advance

Use a defined decision context and specific supplied evidence. Ask students to identify
assumptions, limitations and trade-offs or to evaluate AI-generated analysis where this aligns

be relied upon as the principal safeguard.
Written exam Online, open book, long
duration (1, 2 or 3 days)
Essays A short piece of writing on a specific
topic or subject.
A long assessment window can weaken the inference that the submission alone evidences
the student's capability. Declare AI permissions clearly. Consider a process commentary,
annotated plan or other evidence only where it supports learning, is manageable and is
specified in advance. Any oral component should be proportionate, accessible, consistently
applied and locally approved.
Written exam Online, open book, long
duration (1, 2 or 3 days)
Case study response Students respond to a real-world
problem or challenge; this may
involve material released in advance
which the student has the opportunity
to review and prepare notes on.
Where primary data collection, fieldwork or local evidence is part of the intended learning,
require students to interpret that evidence directly. Make assumptions, limitations and
decision-making visible and declare AI permissions clearly. A reflective process section can
add context but does not by itself prove authorship.
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Take-away or take-home
essays
Essays written after teaching has
been completed and in response to a
list of questions or titles released at a
scheduled time. Students typically
have around a week to select title(s)
and complete their work.
Declare AI permissions clearly before the assessment window opens. Strengthen the task
through engagement with supplied or course-specific evidence, a justified critical position and
marking criteria that reward reasoning. Use annotated source notes, plans or process
evidence only where proportionate. A brief oral follow-up is one possible form of triangulation
if planned, accessible, consistently applied and locally approved.
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Case studies Students find, select and argue for
solutions to a real-world problem or
challenge and either write up or
orally present their findings.
Use a specific case, decision context and supplied evidence. Ask for a prioritised
recommendation, explicit reasoning, assumptions and trade-offs. Process documentation or
an oral presentation may add evidence where it supports the intended learning and is
proportionate, accessible and approved. Declare any permitted AI use and the required
declaration.

Formats 21-25
Format | Type | Task | Description | Proportionate direct-evidence design strategies
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Practical reports Concise documents used to
communicate the outcomes of an
experiment or small-scale project.
Tie the report to the relevant experimental or practical evidence and ask students to justify
methodology, interpretation and limitations. Raw data, notebooks or supervisor records can
provide context but are not proof of authorship. Declare permitted AI use for analysis,
visualisation or writing support and specify how outputs must be checked.
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Poster assessments A form of visual communication
which requires students to concisely
summarise and prioritise information
and/or ideas.
Clarify whether the poster assesses content, visual communication, oral response or all three.
Use a live question-and-answer element only where oral explanation is part of the intended
learning and it can be consistently and accessibly implemented. Include original or supplied
evidence where relevant and use reflective process evidence proportionately. Declare AI
permissions.
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Business plans and
design proposals
Documents which set out to
persuade potential funders of the
feasibility and profitability of a new
product or service and how this will
be achieved.
Use a defined client brief, evidence base and decision context. Assess feasibility, trade-offs
and justification rather than polish alone. An oral pitch, process documentation or client
interaction may provide additional evidence where aligned, accessible and approved. Do not
rely on the scenario being unfamiliar to AI. Declare permitted AI use and verification
responsibilities.
Submission Short duration (typically 5
days to 3 weeks)
Translations Assess competence in translation as
well as understanding and application
of linguistic and stylistic features in a
modern or classical language.
Choose source texts appropriate to the intended translation capability and ask for
commentary explaining linguistic and stylistic choices. Declare AI permissions clearly in
advance. Restricted, unpublished or recently published status may support assessment
quality but should not be treated as proof that AI assistance was unavailable.
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Dissertation / Thesis Extensive pieces of work based on
supervised independent research or
investigation. The title and focus are
often developed by the student. Often
completed at the culmination of the
degree programme.
Declare AI permissions for the specific work and stage. Use supervision and milestones as
developmental evidence, not forensic proof. Require primary sources, original data or
fieldwork where these are part of the intended research capability. A well-designed viva can
provide strong evidence of understanding, but it is not a universal requirement or proof of
authorship. Require the locally prescribed declaration.

Formats 26-30
Format | Type | Task | Description | Proportionate direct-evidence design strategies
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Extended essays A longer essay written on a topic of
the student's choice. Can be written
in response to pre-set questions, or
on a title the student has developed.
Students usually receive limited
supervision and may be entitled to
feedback on draft work.
Align the title and task with the student's intended research and disciplinary context. Use
research notes, annotated sources or drafts only where they support development and
assessment validity. Declare AI permissions clearly. A brief oral follow-up may provide
additional evidence if planned, accessible, consistently applied and locally approved.
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Mini-projects and
structured projects
Students are challenged to address
more open-ended questions, extend
a known result or work with an
industry partner on a problem.
Mini-projects are typically released
after teaching is completed;
structured projects run over a longer
period with supervision.
Use notebooks, code, design records or process logs where they support the intended
learning and remain manageable. Regular check-ins and milestone reviews can make
development visible but do not prove authorship. Declare AI permissions; where AI tools are
permitted, require critical evaluation, testing and an appropriate declaration.
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Fieldwork, site, museum
and industrial visit
reports
Assess students' learning through
fieldwork, a site or museum visit, or
an industrial visit. Generally
presented in a clearly structured
format.
Tie the task to the relevant fieldwork, visit or situated experience. Use dated notes,
observations, material evidence or reflection where these genuinely evidence the intended
learning, while avoiding unnecessary surveillance. Declare AI permissions for the write-up
phase and make clear that contemporaneous records provide context rather than proof of
authorship.
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Portfolio A collection of shorter pieces of work
chosen by the student to
demonstrate their competence and
interest in a particular area.
Ask students to explain the selection, development and relationship of portfolio pieces and to
reflect on their learning trajectory. Include drafts or iterations where they support the intended
learning and are manageable. Mark curation, evidence and judgement rather than assuming
personal reflection establishes authorship. Declare AI permissions clearly.
Submission Long duration (typically
more than 3 weeks)
Research papers and
articles
Work follows the format and
conventions of a published paper or
article in the field, often following the
brief of a particular journal.
Where appropriate to the learning outcome, use original data, primary research or a clearly
justified argument. Peer review, oral feedback or staged development can provide additional
evidence if proportionate and planned. Declare AI permissions for literature work, analysis,
visualisation and drafting and require critical evaluation of substantive outputs.

Translations Assess competence in translation as

modern or classical language.
Choose source texts appropriate to the intended capability and ask for a substantial
translator's commentary that makes linguistic judgement visible. Supervision or milestone
records can add developmental evidence but do not prove authorship. Declare AI permissions
for both translation and commentary phases.
Oral, practical
or performed
Practical tests and
simulations
Various Students are observed conducting a
series of defined tasks within a
specified period of time and are
assessed against a set of
pre-determined criteria.
Observed practical performance can provide strong direct evidence of physical, procedural
and real-time decision-making capability. Use suitable scenario variation and standardised
criteria for assessment quality and fairness. Ensure observation is accessible and consistent,
and declare any relevant AI permissions, such as approved AI-assisted diagnostic tools.
Oral, practical
or performed
Oral presentation In relation to a case study,
research project or paper
Assesses knowledge and
understanding, as well as the ability
to communicate orally and visually.
The audience may include peers and
members of the department. May be
performed live or pre-recorded.
Live delivery and responsive questioning can strengthen evidence of individual understanding
and communication. For pre-recorded work, consider a planned live follow-up only where oral
response is part of the intended learning and it can be accessible and consistently applied.
Declare AI permissions for preparation and require an appropriate declaration for substantive
use.
Oral, practical
or performed
Viva voce and interviews In relation to a
dissertation or thesis
Oral examination of a student's
understanding of submitted written
work and its wider implications. The
format may be formal and structured
or less formal and dialogic.
A well-designed oral examination can provide strong evidence of individual understanding in
real time. Use structured core questions with responsive follow-up to probe reasoning, wider
implications and application, while maintaining fairness and accessibility. Declare any AI
permissions relating to the submitted work under examination.

Oral, practical

Performances, exhibitions
and displays
Musical performance Assesses practical application of
understanding and knowledge.
Allows students to communicate their
creative practice to wider audiences.
Live performance can provide strong direct evidence of the assessed practical or artistic
capability. Use programme choice, interpretation or improvisation where these align with the
learning outcome. For pre-recorded work, consider a planned live commentary or defence
only where proportionate and accessible. Declare AI permissions for related composition or
arrangement work.
Oral, practical
or performed
Performances, exhibitions
and displays
Exhibition of created work Assessment of original creative work
exhibited for an audience.
Use a process portfolio, sketches, drafts or studio notes where they support the intended
creative learning. A live commentary or defence may add evidence of the student's choices if
planned, accessible and consistently applied; it does not by itself prove authorship. Declare AI
permissions and distinguish substantive AI-generated or AI-modified elements where
required.
Oral, practical
or performed
Objective Structured
Clinical Examinations
(OSCEs)
Various Students undertake a series of tasks
in sequence to demonstrate a range
of skills and competencies in
authentic contexts.
The real-time, multi-station and physical format can provide strong evidence of the assessed
clinical capabilities. Use standardised criteria, suitable scenario variation and trained
assessors to support validity and fairness. Declare any AI permissions relevant to preparatory
simulations or tools used within stations.
Oral, practical
or performed
Aural examination Various A series of exercises that assess
listening skills, typically in relation to
language or musical competency.
Live or closely invigilated conditions can provide strong evidence of listening and spontaneous
response. Use varied stimulus material and appropriate spontaneous tasks for assessment
quality. Declare and implement the AI permission clearly, including any authorised assistive
technology or reasonable adjustments.
Oral, practical
or performed
Oral examination Various A series of exercises that assess
verbal skills, typically in relation to
language competency.
Live, controlled oral conditions can provide strong evidence of spontaneous language
production. Use varied topics, registers and responsive prompts with consistent criteria.
Declare and implement the AI permission clearly. Where pre-recorded elements are used, a
planned live follow-up may be appropriate if spontaneity is part of the intended learning and
the component is accessible and approved.
Implementation note. Apply current Oxford Examination Regulations, the University's AI-use policy, local programme guidance, accessibility requirements, reasonable adjustments and student workload expectations. The format
is only one part of validity; alignment, implementation, marking and the evidence students are asked to show are equally important.
