                                   assessment types rather than prescribing one University-wide design.



A S S E S S M E N T- D E S I G N P R I N C I P L E


Design assessments that produce verifiable judgement.
The central question is not simply "Did AI write this?" but whether the assessment provides credible

evidence that the student can reach a reasoned conclusion, and explain, defend and apply it. VIVA is
the practical framework for designing that evidence.




The five-step workflow

  1      Clarify the purpose
         State the knowledge, reasoning, judgement, performance or professional capability the assessment
         should evidence.


  2      Choose the permission level
         Decide whether AI is Not Permitted, Limited, Open or Integral. These are toolkit categories for
         communication, not mandatory University-wide labels.

  5      Mark, review and improve

         evidence, student experience or technology changes.

     Permission levels are a shared language, not an assessment design in themselves.
     Their value depends on alignment with the intended learning, disciplinary context,
     equitable access, staff capacity, marking and workable implementation.




      I M P L E M E N TAT I O N S A F E G U A R D S

      Process evidence is evidence, not proof of authorship. Oral follow-up is not a universal gold standard.
      Where AI could materially obscure the capability being assessed, draw on more than one
      proportionate source of evidence, such as the submitted work together with staged drafts, explanation

      or dialogue. Triangulation strengthens the judgement; no single component proves authorship.

      Any oral or additional component that affects marks should be specified in advance, consistently
      applied, accessible, proportionate and locally approved. Do not selectively viva only students

      suspected of AI use.

Minimum quality-assurance check
Seven statements that should be true before the assessment is issued.


     The intended learning and the reason for the AI permission level are explicit.

     Students receive written guidance before the assessment and know what to declare.

     Permitted tools are equitably available, or an appropriate alternative is provided.

     The design is accessible, proportionate and manageable for students, markers and administrators.


     Marking criteria reward the intended knowledge, reasoning, judgement or performance.

     No claim is made that a format, process log or oral component proves authorship or is AI-proof.

     The design has the necessary local approval and fits Examination Regulations and programme requirements.
