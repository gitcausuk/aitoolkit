Extract the whole ZIP, then open index.html in a current browser.
For the guided tool, open assessment-designer/index.html.
Keep the directory structure intact. A ZIP, Teams or SharePoint preview may not execute HTML.

This revision preserves the existing branding and accessibility implementation.
The logos, palette, fonts, CSS, layout, accessibility statement, accessible text
alternatives, PDF guides and keyboard-navigation routines were not redesigned.

What improved
- Assessment advice no longer treats process records, writing style or a single
  oral exchange as proof of authorship. Several policy/approval overclaims were corrected.
- The Designer starts independently of optional external document readers.
- Uploaded brief text, local guidance, learning outcomes and constraints travel
  into the educator draft. Long excerpts are explicitly labelled if shortened.
- Selected evidence and permissions are respected; studying AI does not trigger
  an automatic requirement to use an AI tool.
- Workload and no-oral constraints are better reflected in the options.
- Generated marking weights are explicitly illustrative, not approved replacements.
- Public policy and current CTL hub links are included in report exports.

Important limits
The Designer uses rules, keyword matching and templates, not an AI model. It
cannot fully interpret or reconcile local regulations, preserve an approved rubric
semantically, validate learning outcomes, certify accessibility or approve a design.
The educator must edit the draft and resolve every local decision before release.

Text/paste and rule-based generation do not require an AI service. Optional PDF
and DOCX readers, online fonts/resources and the optional AI Adviser may require
network access. If a file reader is unavailable, paste the text or use TXT.
The optional Adviser is a separate service, not part of the local rule-based engine.
