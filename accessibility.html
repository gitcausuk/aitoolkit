Accessibility statement

This statement applies to the Oxford AI Toolkit website and its AI Designer. The Toolkit is maintained by the Head of Teaching and Learning at Saïd Business School, University of Oxford.

Our aim

We want as many people as possible to be able to use this website. You should be able to:

  • navigate the site and operate its tools using a keyboard;
  • use current screen-reading and speech-recognition software;
  • zoom and reflow content without losing essential information or functionality;
  • change browser text spacing, colours and fonts; and
  • understand controls, headings, status messages and form instructions.

AbilityNet provides guidance on adapting your device to meet your needs.

Compliance status

Partially compliant with WCAG 2.2 AA. The HTML pages and interactive tools have been designed and checked against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines version 2.2, Level AA. The downloadable PDF documents are not fully tagged; equivalent text versions are provided beside each local PDF download.

Known accessibility limitations

Downloadable PDF documents

The three Toolkit PDFs contain selectable text but are not tagged for reliable structural navigation by a screen reader. This may fail WCAG 2.2 success criteria 1.3.1 (Info and Relationships) and 4.1.2 (Name, Role, Value). A plain-text alternative is provided beside each PDF download. Contact us if you need a different accessible format.

Third-party content

The Toolkit links to Oxford and third-party websites and services. We cannot control the accessibility of those external platforms, but we aim to identify external links and use accessible link text. Content processed by the AI Designer remains in the browser; locally hosted document-reading libraries are used.

Feedback and alternative formats

If you find an accessibility problem, need information in a different format, or believe this statement is inaccurate, contact us. We will consider the request and aim to respond within 10 working days.

Enforcement procedure

The Equality and Human Rights Commission is responsible for enforcing the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018. If you are not satisfied with our response, you can contact the Equality Advisory and Support Service.

How we tested the site

The package was reviewed on 12 August 2026 using source inspection, automated WCAG checks across the main rendered views and AI Designer, keyboard-pattern review, colour-contrast calculations, heading and landmark checks, form-label checks, link and resource checks, and responsive-code inspection. Automated testing cannot establish complete conformance, so formal deployment should also include testing in supported browsers with NVDA, VoiceOver or an equivalent screen reader, keyboard-only use at 200% and 400% zoom, and review by SBS Communications or the responsible digital-accessibility owner.

Preparation of this statement

This statement was prepared on 12 August 2026. It should be reviewed after material changes to the site and at least annually.

For the University’s wider approach, see the University of Oxford accessibility statement and the Saïd Business School website policies.
