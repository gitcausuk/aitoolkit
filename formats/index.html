Written exams

    A short piece of writing on a specific topic or subject.

  • Multiple choice questions — In-person, timed invigilated

    Students are required to select the correct answer from a list of possible answers.

  • Short-answer questions — In-person, timed invigilated

    Open-ended questions that assess knowledge and understanding. Students may be required to complete a sentence, provide a short descriptive answer or annotate a diagram.

  • Problem / computational question — In-person, timed invigilated

Submitted work

  • Take-away or take-home essays — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    Essays written after teaching has been completed and in response to a list of questions or titles released at a scheduled time. Students typically have around a week to select title(s) and complete their work.

  • Case studies — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    Students find, select and argue for solutions to a real-world problem or challenge and either write up or orally present their findings.

  • Practical reports — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    Concise documents used to communicate the outcomes of an experiment or small-scale project.

  • Poster assessments — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    A form of visual communication which requires students to concisely summarise and prioritise information and/or ideas.

  • Business plans and design proposals — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    Documents which set out to persuade potential funders of the feasibility and profitability of a new product or service and how this will be achieved.

  • Translations — Short duration (typically five days to three weeks)

    Assess competence in translation as well as understanding and application of linguistic and stylistic features in a modern or classical language.

  • Dissertation / thesis — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Extensive pieces of work based on supervised independent research or investigation. The title and focus are often developed by the student. Often completed at the culmination of the degree programme.

  • Extended essays — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    A longer essay written on a topic of the student's choice. Can be written in response to pre-set questions or on a title the student has developed. Students usually receive limited supervision and may be entitled to feedback on draft work.

  • Mini-projects and structured projects — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Students are challenged to address more open-ended questions, extend a known result or work with an industry partner on a problem. Mini-projects are typically released after teaching is completed; structured projects run over a longer period with supervision.

  • Fieldwork, site, museum and industrial visit reports — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Assess students' learning through fieldwork, a site or museum visit or an industrial visit. Generally presented in a clearly structured format.

  • Portfolio — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    A collection of shorter pieces of work chosen by the student to demonstrate their competence and interest in a particular area.

  • Research papers and articles — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Work follows the format and conventions of a published paper or article in the field, often following the brief of a particular journal.

  • Business plans and design proposals — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Documents which set out to persuade potential funders of the feasibility and profitability of a new product or service and how this will be achieved.

  • Translations — Long duration (typically more than three weeks)

    Assess competence in translation as well as understanding and application of linguistic and stylistic features in a modern or classical language.

Oral, practical and performed work

  • Practical tests and simulations

    Students are observed conducting a series of defined tasks within a specified period of time and are assessed against a set of pre-determined criteria.

  • Oral presentation on a case study, research project or paper — Oral presentation

    Assesses knowledge and understanding, as well as the ability to communicate orally and visually. The audience may include peers and members of the department. May be performed live or pre-recorded.

  • Viva voce and interviews

    Oral examination of a student's understanding of submitted written work and its wider implications. The format may be formal and structured or less formal and dialogic.

  • Musical performance — Performances, exhibitions and displays

    Assesses practical application of understanding and knowledge. Allows students to communicate their creative practice to wider audiences.

  • Exhibition of created work — Performances, exhibitions and displays

    Assessment of original creative work exhibited for an audience.

  • Objective structured clinical examinations (OSCEs)

    Students undertake a series of tasks in sequence to demonstrate a range of skills and competencies in authentic contexts.

  • Aural examination

    A series of exercises that assess listening skills, typically in relation to language or musical competency.

  • Oral examination

    A series of exercises that assess verbal skills, typically in relation to language competency.

No format, process record or oral component automatically proves authorship or eliminates unauthorised assistance. Where AI could obscure the capability being assessed, use more than one proportionate source of evidence. Make additional components accessible, consistently applied and locally approved.

These suggestions support local decisions. They do not change University policy or permit AI use. Follow the assessment instructions, current Oxford Examination Regulations and local programme guidance. Agree changes, reasonable adjustments and any additional assessment components in advance.

Check the University policy on AI use in summative assessment and Oxford guidance on generative AI.
